# Sources

- Sid Meier, "Interesting Decisions" (GDC 2012, Firaxis) - https://gdcvault.com/play/1015756/Interesting - supports: gameplay as a series of interesting decisions - checked 2026-11-14
- Jaime Griesemer, interview "Half-minute Halo" (Engadget, 2011-07-14) - https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-14-half-minute-halo-an-interview-with-jaime-griesemer.html - supports: the 30-seconds-of-fun idea is Griesemer's (Bungie); he says it is misread and that the point was varying context (environments, weapons, vehicles, enemies) - checked 2026-11-14
- Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses (Morgan Kaufmann/Elsevier, 1st ed., 2008), ch. 6-7 "Rule of the Loop", "Eight Filters", "Risk Assessment and Prototyping", "Eight Tips for Productive Prototyping", "Closing the Loop" - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: design as a loop of idea, prototype, test; eight filters; risk-driven prototypes; prototyping rules; toy before game - checked 2026-11-14
- Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design (1st ed., 2008), ch. 8 "The Player" - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: eight pleasures (sensation, fantasy, narrative, challenge, fellowship, discovery, expression, submission) and Bartle's four types as two lists that both leave gaps - checked 2026-11-14
- Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design (1st ed., 2008), ch. 9 "The Experience" - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: flow needs challenge to grow with skill, zig-zag rise, flow shows only in long observation - checked 2026-11-14
- Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design (1st ed., 2008), ch. 10-11 "Some Elements Are Game Mechanics", "Game Mechanics Must Be in Balance" - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: expected value against perceived probability; twelve balance types; challenge patterns (first levels very easy); dominant options and triangularity; reward types, acclimation and timing; punishment gives rewards value; tune by doubling or halving; three problems of dynamic difficulty; elements scored by purposes served; players should not balance the game - checked 2026-11-14
- Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design (1st ed., 2008), ch. 14 "The Experience Rises in Interest Curves" - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: interest curve with hook, rests and larger finale; flat stretch is a defect - checked 2026-11-14
- Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design (1st ed., 2008), ch. 25 "Good Games Are Created Through Playtesting" - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: playtest plan (why, who, what to log), three least-liked parts, do not defend the game - checked 2026-11-14
- Tracy Fullerton, Game Design Workshop, 5th ed. (A K Peters/CRC Press, 2024) - https://www.routledge.com/Game-Design-Workshop-A-Playcentric-Approach-to-Creating-Innovative-Games/Fullerton/p/book/9781032607009 - supports: edition, publisher, year - checked 2026-11-14
- Tracy Fullerton, "Book Excerpt: Game Design Workshop" (Game Developer, 2024) - https://gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-design-workshop - supports: experience goals name the type of experience players will have; build a playable version right after brainstorming; playtest early to find flaws before they cost more; iterate (set goals, prototype, test, evaluate, fix, repeat) until the design meets the goals - checked 2026-11-14
- Celia Hodent, "The Gamer's Brain, part 3" (GDC 2017 talk post) - https://celiahodent.com/gamers-brain-part-3-ux-engagement-immersion-retention-gdc17-talk/ - supports: engageability as motivation, emotion and game flow; sawtooth difficulty; three causes of unintended behavior (missing design, poor balance, poor communication) - checked 2026-11-14
- Celia Hodent, "Ethics in the videogame industry" - https://celiahodent.com/ethics-in-the-videogame-industry/ - supports: avoid punishing disengagement, for example consecutive daily-login requirements - checked 2026-11-14
- Ian Schreiber, "Level 7: Advancement, Progression, and Pacing" (Game Balance Concepts, 2010) - https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/08/18/level-7-advancement-progression-and-pacing/ - supports: perceived difficulty formula; PvE sawtooth pacing; reward spacing (too few, too many, random schedule tied to player action) - checked 2026-11-14
- Ian Schreiber, "Level 5: Probability and Randomness Gone Horribly Wrong" (Game Balance Concepts, 2010) - https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/08/04/level-5-probability-and-randomness-gone-horribly-wrong/ - supports: players expect streaks to break; six losses in a row at the start reads as unfair - checked 2026-11-14
- Steve Swink, Game Feel (Morgan Kaufmann/Elsevier, 1st ed., 2009), ch. 1-2, 4-5, 7, 9, 11, 17 - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/game-feel/swink/978-0-12-374328-2 - supports: three-part test (real-time control, simulated space, polish); latency and frame-rate thresholds; lag measurement; velocity against position movement; response envelope; deterministic rule for simultaneous inputs; polish as signals; rules change perceived solidity; test control with the world removed - checked 2026-11-14
- Richard A. Bartle, Designing Virtual Worlds (New Riders, 2003), ch. 3 "Player Types" - https://mud.co.uk/dvw/ - supports: type dynamics (more killers, fewer socializers), the model is descriptive, achievers leave without intervention, newbie flow and gross churn - checked 2026-11-14
