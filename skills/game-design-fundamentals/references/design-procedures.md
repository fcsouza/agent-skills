# Design procedures

Long forms for `game-design-fundamentals`. Sources for each line are in `references/sources.md`.

## Prototype rules

1. Every prototype answers a stated question.
2. Do not polish. Do not get attached.
3. Order prototypes by risk, then by dependency (upstream first).
4. Run several in parallel.
5. Paper prototypes can stand in for real-time games.
6. Use a tool that lets you change values while the game runs.
7. Test whether the thing is fun to play with before adding a goal: do people start touching it before they know the rules?

## Eight filters for a candidate design

Run the idea through each: artistic impulse, demographics, experience design, innovation, business and marketing, engineering, community, playtesting. A fix for one filter can break another, so re-run all eight after each change. You may change a filter (for example the target audience) instead of the design.

## Balance checklist

Twelve balance questions: fairness; challenge against success; meaningful choices; skill against chance; head against hands; competition against cooperation; short against long; rewards; punishment; freedom against control; simple against complex; detail against imagination.

- Fairness: symmetric games give equal powers and measure skill. Asymmetric games need more tuning. Score each trait, guess weights, then adjust until totals match observed play. Rock-paper-scissors cycles stop a supreme option.
- Challenge: raise difficulty after each success, let strong players pass easy parts fast, add optional layers, playtest with novices and experts.
- Length: session length follows the end conditions. Change win and lose rules to tune it.
- Tuning method: change by doubling or halving. Write down your model of how values relate and revise it after each experiment.

## Interest curve

Plot interest over the game, each level and each challenge. A good curve has a hook, rising interest with short rests, and a finale larger than all before it. Draw one per player type. Ask playtesters to draw theirs. Too much interest early diminishes later peaks.

## Playtest plan

- Why: a specific question, never "is it fun".
- Who: developers, friends, experts and fresh testers are each biased differently.
- What to log: in-game events such as time in a menu or the weapons used.
- Ask for three least-liked parts. Do not ask testers to design. Watch faces as well as the screen.

## Newbie flow (online games)

A live world leaks players. Average inflow must at least equal outflow. Measure gross churn (the monthly percentage who leave), not only net growth, because heavy recruitment hides churn.
