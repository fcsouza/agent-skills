# Sources

- Jesse Schell, "The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses" (Morgan Kaufmann / Elsevier, 1st ed., 2008), ch. 15 (story methods and story tips) - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: string of pearls versus story machine; protagonist goals and obstacles matching the player's challenges; Hero's Journey as a skeleton, not a formula; the questions on which conflicts let different stories arise - checked 2026-11-14
- Jesse Schell, "The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses" (Morgan Kaufmann / Elsevier, 1st ed., 2008), ch. 15, section "The Problems" - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: branching stories lose unity; 10 levels of 3 choices give 88,573 outcomes; convergence makes early choices less meaningful; multiple endings make players doubt which is real and dread replaying, with two full stories (light and dark side) as the exception; few verbs for story action; undo removes tragedy - checked 2026-11-14
- Ken Levine, "Narrative Legos" (GDC 2014, Irrational Games) - https://gdcvault.com/play/1021234/Narrative - supports: the talk argues for breaking narrative into small elements that recombine into non-linear, replayable sequences - checked 2026-11-14
