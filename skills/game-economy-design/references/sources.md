# Sources

- Soren Johnson, designer-notes.com column (Game Developer magazine, March 2011; posted 2011-06-12) - https://www.designer-notes.com/?p=369 - supports: the line that players, given the chance, will optimize the fun out of a game, and that Johnson (not Koster) wrote it - checked 2026-11-14
- Ian Schreiber, "Game Balance Concepts, Level 10: Final Boss" (2010) - https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/09/08/level-10-final-boss/ - supports: money sinks (NPC purchases, upkeep); the two fixes for inflation (cut positive-sum sources or add negative-sum sinks); track the total money in the game - checked 2026-11-14
- Ian Schreiber, "Level 2: Numeric Relationships" (2010) - https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/07/14/level-2-numeric-relationships/ - supports: exponential doubling costs 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and gets large fast - checked 2026-11-14
- Ian Schreiber, "Level 6: Situational Balance" (2010) - https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/08/11/level-6-situational-balance/ - supports: price a situational benefit by multiplying it by how often the situation occurs - checked 2026-11-14
- Ian Schreiber, "Level 8: Metrics and Statistics" (2010) - https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/08/25/level-8-metrics-and-statistics/ - supports: ARPU and ARPPU show whether the problem is acquisition, conversion or retention; median below mean signals a skewed distribution - checked 2026-11-14
- Raph Koster, "The Laws of Online World Design" - https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/ - supports: a faucet-and-drain economy spawns new stuff and needs a matching drain - checked 2026-11-14
- Raph Koster, "The Rules of Online World Design" - https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-rules-of-online-world-design/ - supports: the Habitat vending-machine loophole gave infinite tokens and bloated the database; "If it can be abused, it will be" - checked 2026-11-14
- Brendan Drain, "EVE Evolved: ISK sinks and faucets" (Engadget, 2010) - https://www.engadget.com/2010/10/24/eve-evolved-isk-sinks-and-faucets/ - supports: a faucet generates currency and does not just transfer it between players; the balance of sinks and faucets sets the inflation rate; faucet and sink examples - checked 2026-11-14
- Edward Castronova, "Virtual Worlds: A First-Hand Account of Market and Society on the Cyberian Frontier" (CESifo Working Paper 618, 2001) - https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo_wp618.pdf - supports: a 29-item price index in EverQuest fell from 100 in Q4 2000 to 71 in Q3 2001, a 29 percent deflation in one year (the cesifo.org URL redirects here) - checked 2026-11-14
- Richard Bartle, Designing Virtual Worlds (New Riders, 2003), ch. 4 "World Design" (Closed or Open Economic Model?; Economics; Interference in Economies; Tips for a Successful Virtual Economy) and ch. 5 "Life in the Virtual World" (offline activity) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designing_Virtual_Worlds - supports: closed economies are hard to balance and none built for a large world; fixed prices drift to free with unbounded money; reward goods over cash; players leave and gouge; duping through stacks and templates; sinks (wear-out items, priced services, exchange fees); cap offline activity; selling power needs careful balancing - checked 2026-11-14
- Jesse Schell, The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses (Morgan Kaufmann / Elsevier, 2008, 1st ed.), ch. 11, section "Balancing Game Economies" - https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-art-of-game-design/schell/978-0-12-369496-6 - supports: an economy is the two choices of how to earn and how to spend, and any tradable thing counts as money - checked 2026-11-14
